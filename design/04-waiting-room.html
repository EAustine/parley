Allow and Deny sit side by side, and the participant row's actions do not. A roster row is passive — it exists to be read, and a stray press acts on someone you were only looking at. A waiting request exists solely to be answered, both answers are expected, and Deny is reversible. Allow is primary, Deny is outlined and quieter, both on the 44px floor, neither revealed on hover.

Every name says where it came from. Signing in buys accountability, not authorisation — anyone can sign in with any account — so a guest can type your name and sit in the list looking like you. Signed in and Guest are both marked, so neither is the unmarked default that reads as trusted.

Denied and removed carry the same block and different words. Being turned away and being ejected are different experiences, and the product should not tell someone the wrong one happened to them. The fifth ending is §3.4's host ended the meeting, unchanged, with no Rejoin.

Leave is live from the first second. Nobody is made to watch something they cannot interrupt — §3.11's rule about the reconnect countdown, applied to a wait whose length nobody controls.

The Identity column is the load-bearing one. A record listing “Austine Eluro” without saying whether that was attested or typed implies an attestation the product cannot make. Name entered is the honest description of a string somebody typed into a box.

A denied person has no times, and that is the shape of the row rather than missing data. They never joined, so no participant_joined ever fired — which is also the first thing to test against the partial unique index, since it was built for rows that have a join.

Outcome is encoded in fill, not hue: outlined for the ordinary endings, filled for the two the host caused. Removed and denied are records of what happened, not live warnings, so --state-critical would be spending hue on something that is not a warning.

Two homes for one switch

Pre-join is where an instant meeting gets one. The room's overflow menu is where a meeting already running gets one, which is the case that actually happens — you start open, someone unexpected appears, you lock the door.

In pre-join, host only. §3.3 has had no host variant until now.

Ask before guests join You'll approve each guest. People signed in to Parley come straight in.

In the room, from the overflow menu. Same words, same switch.

Ask before guests join 2 people are waiting.

On for scheduled meetings, off for instant ones. Risk tracks how long a link has been in the world: a scheduled link went out days ago to a list nobody re-reads, an instant link was pasted seconds ago to someone already waiting. Reversible in both directions, and worth revisiting once there is any usage to look at.

“Ask before guests join” rather than “Waiting room”. The vocabulary table names things by what they do for the person setting them, and a host toggling this is deciding whether they get asked — not naming a room.
